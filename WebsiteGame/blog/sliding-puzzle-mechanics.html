How 15-Puzzles Work: Solvability Mathematics & Solving Methods

By Deepak Kumar · Published October 2026 · Updated November 2026 · 8 min read

Invented in the late 1870s by Noyes Palmer Chapman and popularized by puzzle pioneer Sam Loyd, the 15-Puzzle (and its 8-puzzle sibling on a 3×3 grid) is one of the most celebrated mechanical challenges in recreational mathematics. On GameHub, we feature both Sliding Puzzle Master and Wooden Number Slide, offering tactile wooden aesthetics and daily streak modes.

Behind the satisfying clack of shifting wooden tiles lies deep abstract algebra. Below, we explain why exactly half of all random permutations can never be solved, and give you a dependable algorithm to solve any scrambled board.

1. The Mathematics of Inversions and Solvability

If you take a physical sliding puzzle apart and pop the tiles back into random slots, there is an exact 50% chance that the puzzle is mathematically unsolvable! In fact, in 1879, mathematician William Woolsey Johnson and story-spinner Sam Loyd offered a \$1,000 bounty to swap tiles 14 and 15 while leaving all other tiles in place—a mathematical impossibility.

What is an Inversion?

An inversion occurs whenever a tile with a greater number precedes a tile with a lesser number in linear reading order (ignoring the empty blank space). For example, if tile 5 appears before tile 2, that constitutes one inversion.

The mathematical theorem for solvability states:

  • On an odd grid (3×3): A state is solvable if and only if the total number of inversions is even.
  • On an even grid (4×4): A state is solvable if and only if the sum of inversions plus the row distance of the blank tile from the bottom is even.

Every legal slide of a tile preserves this mathematical parity invariant! In our digital implementations on GameHub, our scrambler algorithm starts from a solved state and performs legal moves, guaranteeing that every single puzzle you play is 100% solvable.

2. The Row-by-Row Solution Method

The most intuitive and foolproof human algorithm for solving sliding tile puzzles is the Top-Down, Row-by-Row Method:

Step A: Solve the Top Row Except the Last Two Tiles

On a 4×4 board, first position Tile 1, then Tile 2 into their correct final positions. Once placed, never move them again.

Step B: The Corner Pair Trick (Tiles 3 and 4)

Many beginners get stuck trying to place Tile 4 directly when Tile 3 is in place. Instead, use the Corner Pair Trick:

  1. Move Tile 3 into Tile 4's target slot (top-right corner).
  2. Move Tile 4 directly beneath Tile 3.
  3. Rotate the empty space around the corner, which slides Tile 3 into its proper slot and Tile 4 directly behind it!

Step C: Repeat for the Next Row

Repeat this identical process for Row 2 (Tiles 5, 6, 7, and 8). Now half the board is permanently solved!

Step D: Solve the Bottom Two Rows Simultaneously

With only a 2×4 subgrid remaining, do not work horizontally. Instead, solve the left column (Tiles 9 and 13) using the same corner pairing technique rotated vertically. Finally, cycling the remaining 3 tiles (10, 11, 12, 14, 15) easily snaps the entire board into victory.
